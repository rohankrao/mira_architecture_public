Why Build: The Gaps No Existing Toolchain Closes

The mature tools are good, actively maintained, and we integrate them — four capabilities simply do not exist in any of them
Landscape
ⓘ WHAT IT DOES: Positions MIRA against the maintained alternatives (datatrove, Data-Juicer, NeMo Curator, dolma, Daft) rather than around them: adopt their operators, build only what is missing.
◎ WHY IT MATTERS: Re-implementing a solved operator is wasted effort. The case for building rests on four capabilities that no maintained framework provides, each of which is a data-model decision that cannot be bolted on later.
⊘ Missing Everywhere The Reason to Build
  • • Incremental recomputation. Improving one tagger re-runs the corpus everywhere. datatrove resumes failed tasks; it does not recompute only what changed. CocoIndex and Pixeltable do this properly, at RAG scale, not for pretraining corpora.
  • • Stateful operators that absorb new data. Daily arrival should fold into a persistent dedup index, corpus statistics and cluster assignments — with the incremental result provably equal to a full rebuild, and deletions retracting from it. Every tool rebuilds from scratch instead.
  • • One deduplication cascade. The mature pipelines stop at exact, MinHash and substring dedup; semantic deduplication means adding a second framework and reconciling two sets of clusters. We keep exact, fuzzy and semantic in one cascade, over one cluster table.
  • • A query layer over curated data. Every framework filters destructively and writes a new corpus. dolma and DCLM keep quality signals, but no tool answers "10% agriculture, quality > 0.8, 100B tokens" with the decision recorded per row.
  • • Indic correctness as an engine property. The one Indic-first pipeline (AI4Bharat setu) has been unmaintained since May 2024. The best multilingual pipeline strips Unicode combining marks during normalisation — which deletes viramas and matras.
  • • Per-record provenance and consent that survives into derived shards, which is what a takedown obligation actually requires.
⊕ Adopted, Not Rebuilt Integration First
  • • Operators: quality heuristics, exact and fuzzy dedup methods, n-gram decontamination — mature in datatrove, Data-Juicer and dolma, and reachable through adapters rather than rewritten.
  • • Extraction: HTML and PDF via trafilatura, Resiliparse and Docling. We add spans and provenance around them, not another parser.
  • • Models: GlotLID, OpenLID, IndicLID for language identification; Presidio's Indian ID patterns for PII.
  • • Execution and storage: Ray, SkyPilot, DuckDB, Parquet, Lance. We are not writing a scheduler or a table format.
  • • Multimodal: speech, video and image curation already exist at scale; Phase 2 arrives as adapters, not as a second engine.

⚠ Systems Analysis Scaling Bottlenecks in Standard CPython Pipelines (Part 1)

The 4 Critical Bottlenecks (Part 1: GIL & Serialization Tax)

Understanding why standard Python curation pipelines hit throughput limits on massive Indic web and book corpora
Bottlenecks 1 & 2
ⓘ WHAT IT DOES: Analyzes the first two structural bottlenecks of standard data pipelines: CPython interpreter dispatch overhead and unnecessary multi-gigabyte disk serialization.
◎ WHY IT MATTERS: Removing these two bottlenecks is where the throughput gain comes from: cluster runtimes drop from weeks to hours, and the same work fits on far fewer nodes.
⧗ 1. The Python Interpreter & Allocation Tax The Slow Typist
The Core Bottleneck: Beyond Python's Global Interpreter Lock (GIL), object-heavy row-by-row string execution incurs severe interpreter dispatch, allocation churn, and GC overhead.

What Goes Wrong: Traditional CPython data pipelines convert raw text into millions of individual Python string objects, destroying L1/L2 CPU cache residency and wasting up to 80% of runtime solely managing heap allocations.

Standard row loop: Python `for doc in dataset:` loops reach roughly 200–1,000 docs/sec per core, depending on the operator, because of object allocation and interpreter dispatch.
MIRA V2 Solution: Apache Arrow contiguous columnar buffers executed in native Rust/C++. Zero-overhead SIMD vectorization executes on CPU registers with zero interpreter lock contention (even considering Python 3.14 free-threading).
▭ 2. High IPC & Intermediate Disk Amplification The Endless Mover
The Analogy: A worker who packs all belongings into boxes, drives to a new house, unpacks everything, and then immediately packs it all back up to move again.

What Goes Wrong: Pipeline stages that serialize full datasets to disk between each step (e.g. Parquet after cleaning, Parquet after dedup, Parquet after PII) amplify a crawl into several times its size of redundant disk writes.

Baseline Approach: High NVMe write amplification; elevated cloud storage costs; disk space exhaustion on staging partitions.
MIRA V2 Solution: Streaming in-memory record batches with Lance columnar appends. Adding a tag column writes one new column fragment instead of rewriting existing data files. Measured: 4.1–9.9 s for a 1M-row tag append; schema-only manifest updates are ~2 ms. A re-benchmark against tuned Parquet is an M02 deliverable.

The 4 Critical Bottlenecks (Part 2: GPU Starvation & Thread Contention)

Preventing expensive cloud GPU waste and out-of-memory crashes on high-concurrency servers
Bottlenecks 3 & 4
ⓘ WHAT IT DOES: Analyzes bottlenecks 3 and 4: GPUs starved while waiting on slow CPU text regexes, and multi-process thread contention causing out-of-memory panics.
◎ WHY IT MATTERS: A100/H100 GPUs cost $3–$4 per hour. Starving them on CPU text parsing burns budgets with zero return. MIRA decouples CPU filters from GPU actors.
▣ 3. GPU Starvation & Idle Cloud Spend The Idle Ferrari
The Analogy: Buying a $300,000 Ferrari to deliver packages, but it spends 95% of its time stuck behind a horse and buggy in bumper-to-bumper city traffic.

What Goes Wrong: Heavy neural models (e.g. IndicBERT quality scoring) run on GPUs, but data arrives as slow Python streams. Without pipelined prefetching, GPUs can sit idle much of the time waiting for CPU-bound tokenisation and regex filtering to produce the next batch.

Baseline Approach: Continuous GPU allocation during early text filtering yields under 15% GPU utilization.
MIRA V2 Solution: Smart Filter Barriers. Ultra-fast CPU kernels filter low-signal documents early; only dense, high-signal batches are streamed to resident GPU actors.
⛶ 4. Process Memory Replication & OOM Spikes The Kitchen With 4 Cooks
The Problem: Independent process execution models replicate runtime heaps, CUDA driver contexts, and allocator metadata rather than truly sharing resident model weights.

What Goes Wrong: Process-per-worker execution (`multiprocessing.Pool`) rapidly breaks Linux Copy-On-Write (COW) pages during string parsing and tokenization. 64 worker processes replicating 2 GB models trigger catastrophic 128+ GB memory spikes and sudden kernel OOM kills.

Baseline Approach: Replicated worker processes risk host memory exhaustion during long-running multi-day pipelines.
MIRA V2 Solution: Unified shared-memory architecture with Arrow memory-mapped zero-copy buffers and resident GPU actors avoiding process replication.

One Pipeline, Three Places: Eliminating Training/Serving Skew

The classic ML failure — training features built one way, inference features another — applies to corpora too, and nothing in the field addresses it
Parity
ⓘ WHAT IT DOES: Makes one serialised pipeline runnable on a laptop sample, on the cluster at full scale, and against a single record at query time — with the equality of those three enforced by a test rather than assumed.
◎ WHY IT MATTERS: If a corpus is normalised one way and inference input another, tokenisation diverges — worst for Indic scripts, where normalisation changes token boundaries. The model is then quietly worse for a reason no log shows.
▣ 1. Serialise Everything Pipeline Bundle
A recipe alone is not enough; every version that can change a result travels with it:
  • • Operators, parameters, when: predicates and seeds
  • • Kernel, model, language-pack and tokenizer versions
  • • Reference-snapshot ids and the Unicode version behind normalisation
  • • An environment fingerprint, so two machines cannot disagree silently
  • • The sample the bundle was validated against
⚖ 2. Same Results, Any Machine Bit-Identical
  • • Instruction-set parity means a laptop on AVX2 and a server on AVX-512 produce identical bytes — "it worked on my machine" becomes a testable claim.
  • • Deterministic stratified sampling gives a laptop a representative slice with a recorded seed, not whichever shard happened to be first.
  • • bundle verify re-runs the bundle against its own sample in the new environment and diffs column by column, naming the operator that diverged.
◉ 3. One Record, Same Answer Serving & RAG
  • • An apply path for a single record shares the operator implementations with the batch engine rather than reimplementing them — which is how skew normally enters a system.
  • • The skew test is a gate: for a fixed sample, batch results equal the concatenation of single-record results, column by column.
  • • Corpus-scoped operators refuse — deduplication and corpus statistics cannot mean anything for one record, so they error by name instead of returning a different answer.
  • • Evaluation sets and RAG indexing run the same operators, so a benchmark measures the model rather than the pipeline difference.

When It Goes Wrong: Restatement, Late Data and Mutable Sources

A corpus is a living dataset with consumers — the questions that decide whether anyone trusts it in production
Recovery
ⓘ WHAT IT DOES: Treats the failure cases of a daily pipeline as first-class: a bad tagger that already shipped, data arriving late, sources that change records under you, and dependencies that go down mid-run.
◎ WHY IT MATTERS: A wrong dashboard gets noticed. A subtly wrong corpus produces a model that is quietly worse, discovered months later once the compute is spent. Detection is slower here, so correction has to be faster.
↺ 1. A Bad Tagger Already Shipped Minutes, Not a Re-run
  • • Blast radius is a query. Operator fingerprints sit in every run manifest and every release, so "which releases used quality@v3?" is a lookup, not an investigation.
  • • Roll back by pointer. Releases are immutable; the dataset alias moves back to the last good snapshot and consumers follow immediately. The bad release is marked revoked, with the reason recorded.
  • • Fix forward cheaply. Bump the operator version and backfill that column only; retraction repairs the affected statistics without rebuilding them.
  • • Preview before acting. A dry run states which columns will be recomputed, over how many rows, at what cost — and what will not be touched.
  • • Publish the clock. Time from detection to consumers-on-good-data is a tracked number, not a belief.
⏱ 2. Late Data & Upserts Order-Independent
  • • Watermarks advance on discovery, never on a document's own timestamp — so a shard delivered two days late, or a portal republishing old records, is still ingested. Watermarking on fetch_time is the classic way to silently skip data.
  • • Upsert is the default. Positional doc_id means a re-fetch of the same slot updates in place instead of accumulating near-duplicate copies of one page.
  • • Changed content re-tags itself. The content hash is part of the cache key, so an edited document misses the cache while its neighbours do not.
  • • Deletes are tombstones, never silent absences — "removed, and why" is what an audit needs.
⚯ 3. Dependencies Go Down Degrade, Don't Fail
  • • A source that is down is skipped, its watermark left unmoved, and it is reported — one unavailable portal does not fail a run across forty sources.
  • • A source that lies is worse than one that is down. An empty file or an error page saved as data gets curated cheerfully, so per-source volume deviation is checked before content is trusted.
  • • Consumers never see a half-written dataset: atomic commit plus an alias, so a slow reader cannot block a writer and a writer cannot corrupt a reader.
  • • Our own store failing mid-run costs the unfinished shards only — resume is shard-level and verified by a kill -9 test.
